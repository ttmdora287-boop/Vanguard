Disaster Preparedness & Survival

Actionable safety guidelines, go-bag essentials, and emergency protocols

1. Secure Your Living Space

Anchor heavy shelves, cabinets, and electronics to prevent tipping during seismic tremors.

2. Drop, Cover, and Hold On

Instantly drop under sturdy furniture and hold secure until all ground vibration ceases.

3. Pack an Emergency Go-Bag

Maintain 3 days of potable water, non-perishable food, first aid supplies, and flashlights.

Emergency Survival Protocols

Step-by-step instructions for indoor, outdoor, and post-quake scenarios

Indoor Protocol

Stay inside. Move away from glass, windows, and exterior walls. Get under a desk or sturdy table.

If no table is nearby, drop down against an interior wall and cover your head and neck with your arms.
Outdoor Protocol

Move away from buildings, streetlights, utility wires, and overpasses immediately.

Once in the open area, stay there until the shaking stops. The greatest danger exists directly near exterior walls.
Vehicle Protocol

Pull over safely to the side of the road away from bridges, tall trees, and power lines.

Stop as quickly as safety permits and stay inside the vehicle until shaking halts completely. Avoid stopping under overpasses.

Extended Emergency Go-Bag Guide

Your checklist is the starting point. This section explains why each item can be useful and how families can organize supplies.

Water

Keep clean drinking water stored in a practical container. Review and replace stored supplies regularly according to the product instructions.

Hydration planning
Food

Choose easy-to-store, non-perishable food that your household can safely eat and rotate supplies before they expire.

Simple & practical
First-aid supplies

Keep a clearly labelled kit and make sure responsible adults know where it is stored and how to get additional help.

Health support
Light & batteries

A flashlight helps families move around safely during a power interruption. Store compatible batteries safely and check equipment.

Visibility
Whistle & communication

A whistle can help attract attention when speaking loudly is difficult. This can be especially useful in an emergency plan for children or people with communication difficulties.

Signal for attention
Important information

Keep useful contact details and copies of important documents in a protected location, following your family's privacy needs.

Family information
A simple family rule: know your bag

Every family member should know where the emergency supplies are kept and who is responsible for checking them. Preparation works best when it is practiced before an emergency.

Go-Bag Planning Tips

  1. Choose a bag that is practical for your household.
  2. Keep it in a location that responsible family members can find.
  3. Check supplies regularly and replace items when needed.
  4. Practice your family plan so everyone knows what to do.

Preparedness by Age Categories

Tailored safety measures for children, adults, elderly persons, and individuals with disabilities

Children & Kids

Children should carry a personal whistle attached to their backpack so they can signal if trapped or unable to shout. Practice regular Drop, Cover, and Hold drills.

Why it reduces risk: Instills calm muscle memory and speeds up rescue location.

Adults & Guardians

Adults must keep mobile phones charged and notifications enabled to receive real-time seismic emergency alerts. Know how to shut off home gas and electricity mains.

Why it reduces risk: Prevents secondary fires and ensures immediate family coordination.

Elderly Citizens

Keep mobility aids (canines, walkers) anchored near beds. Pre-arrange designated family check-in buddies and ensure prescription medicines are in the Go-Bag.

Why it reduces risk: Mitigates mobility hurdles and guarantees continuous medical care.

Disabled Persons

Establish evacuation buddy systems, identify accessible ground-floor exits, and keep specialized medical equipment strapped securely against wall studs.

Why it reduces risk: Ensures no individual is left behind during emergency evacuations.

Preparedness Across Different Workplaces & Public Areas

How schools, hotels, supermarkets, offices, and public spaces prepare to reduce earthquake risk

Schools & Educational Institutions

Conduct monthly earthquake simulation drills. Secure heavy classroom lockers, bookshelves, and overhead projector screens to walls.

Risk Reduction Impact: Protects students from falling furniture and ensures orderly assembly in school courtyards.

Hotels & Hospitality Areas

Place clear multi-lingual evacuation maps in every guest room. Anchor large mirrors and TVs. Train front desk staff on emergency PA broadcast protocols.

Risk Reduction Impact: Guides international guests safely out of unfamiliar buildings without panic.

Supermarkets & Retail Spaces

Install bungee cords or safety lips across high merchandise shelves to prevent heavy canned goods or bottles from raining down on shoppers during shaking.

Risk Reduction Impact: Prevents massive retail aisle injuries and maintains clear aisle exit paths.

Corporate Offices & Workplaces

Designate floor safety wardens, establish automated emergency lighting backups, and keep heavy filing cabinets bolted together.

Risk Reduction Impact: Safeguards staff members and ensures rapid coordination to municipal assembly stadiums.

Community Support & Recovery Planning

Preparedness also includes planning how communities can support one another after an emergency.

Assembly-area preparation

Tents & shelter

Food & water

Organize supplies through responsible community systems.

Backpacks & essentials

Information desk

Share verified instructions and family support information.

Donation & support campaign

A community campaign can encourage people and organizations with resources to support verified relief efforts. Donations should be coordinated through trusted schools, community groups, charities, or official emergency organizations.

  • Create a clear list of needed supplies.
  • Explain where verified donations can be delivered.
  • Keep records transparent and organized.
  • Prioritize the needs identified by affected communities.
